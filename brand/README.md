# glassgarden brand kit

glassgarden is generative ambient music, grown in small sealed worlds: a few rules, patched between small instruments or written as code, then left to run. The identity is a single glass jar drawn on a pixel grid, with a little amber light drifting out of the lid. It pairs old technology with something alive, and it keeps a bit of nerdiness: every jar grows from a seed, and the site's jar grows from a new one on every visit.

There are two editions of the same idea.

- **Quiet** (`logos/quiet`, `social/quiet`) is the main identity. Use it by default: the website, Bandcamp, streaming profiles, press, release covers.
- **Pixel** (`logos/pixel`, `social/pixel`) is the original pixel-font edition. Keep it for places where it feels at home: the norns and monome community, stickers, merch, gig visuals and projections, playful one-offs.

The pixel jar belongs to both. What makes the pixel edition playful is the pixel lettering and the shelf-of-jars scene, so those stay out of quiet pieces. Don't mix the two editions in one piece.

## Name

- Always one lowercase word: **glassgarden**. Never "Glass Garden" or "GlassGarden".
- In the quiet edition the name is set in IBM Plex Mono Light, followed by an amber dot: **glassgarden.** The dot is the only color in the wordmark.
- Release titles are lowercase too: `terrarium`, `pollen`.
- Line: **generative ambient · grown in small sealed worlds**. Say what the music is made from (rules, small instruments, code), never what it isn't.

## Handles

The plain name is taken on most platforms, so the display name stays **glassgarden** everywhere and the handle is the domain without its dot, the same on every platform: **glassgardencc**. GitHub is the one exception, where the plain name was free: **glassgarden**. The amber dot in the wordmark already points at the domain: glassgarden. → glassgarden.cc.

The cc stands for **control change**, the MIDI message for turning a knob on something that's already playing. It's what the music asks of me: not writing notes, just nudging a running system. Use that one meaning and don't stack others on it. It's explained once, on the About page. Elsewhere, let people find it.

- Below about 200px, use the small mark. The fine jar's one-pixel lines and dark moss disappear at avatar sizes.
- Scale the pixel art in whole steps and keep `image-rendering: pixelated` so edges stay crisp.
- Leave clear space around the lockup at least as wide as the jar's lid.
- Don't recolor, outline, rotate, blur or add effects. Choose the night or day file instead.

## Type

- **IBM Plex Mono Light** for the name, page titles and release titles.
- **IBM Plex Mono Regular** for small labels, dates and metadata, often in uppercase with wide letter spacing.
- **IBM Plex Sans** (Light for large text, Regular for body) for reading text.
- All free on Google Fonts. The pixel edition uses its own hand-drawn pixel letters (in the SVGs) and Silkscreen for any extra pixel text.

## Voice

- Quiet and specific. Say what a piece does, not how it should make someone feel. "A slow pulse divides into four voices that drift in and out of phase", not "an immersive sonic journey".
- First person, plain words, no hype, no emoji.
- The garden is the frame: planting, growing, seeds and jars are fine for names, headings and small asides ("nothing grew here"). When describing a piece, talk about the music.

## Photos

Real things, sparingly: hands and instruments, patches, the places where pieces get made. No posed portraits; from behind or at a distance is fine.

- Every photo gets the same grade, on the site and on social: 4:5, colour muted to about half, a little warm, blacks lifted to `ground` and whites held at `ink`. No filters, grain, vignettes or glows.
- `generator/grade.py` does all of it and writes a WebP with no metadata (no camera details, no GPS):

  ```sh
  python grade.py original.jpg ../../assets/img/photos/name.webp --focus 0.5,0.45
  ```

  `--focus` moves the crop and `--zoom 0.8` tightens it. For instruments, add `--night`: the panels sink into the dark and only the LEDs and screens keep their colour. Crop in close rather than showing the whole case; a corner with lights says more than an inventory.

- Keep originals outside the site folder. Phone photos carry GPS.
- One photo per note at most. Caption in lowercase: where and when, not how it felt.
- Pixel dithering belongs to the pixel edition (posters, gig visuals), not to photos.

One jar, centered on `ground`, grown from that release's seed (the same seed as in the website's `data/releases.js`). Title below in Plex Mono Light, lowercase. Nothing else.

## Regenerating

`generator/gen_brand.py` rebuilds every SVG here, the avatar PNGs and the website's icons, from the same pixel jar the website uses (`generator/gen.py` holds the jar, the pixel font and the palettes). From `brand/generator`:

```sh
pip install pillow fonttools
python gen_brand.py .. IBMPlexMono-Light.ttf ../../assets/img
```

The quiet banner and og image have outlined type, so export their PNGs from the SVGs in a browser at exact size, for example:

```sh
chrome --headless --window-size=1200,630 --screenshot=glassgarden-og-1200x630.png glassgarden-og.svg
```

then copy the og PNG to `assets/img/og-image.png`.

Change the seed in `quiet_jar()` to grow a different jar.
